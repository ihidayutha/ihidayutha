Cure For Myopic View of History

Let me ask it another way, as I have wondered this question before many times in conversation with others who hold similar views as you. 

You mention the name of John Calvin (1509-1564). You seem to know something about at least one other person related to Church History. I'm happy about this and proud of you that you know something about someone like John Calvin, whether or not you or I agree or believe in what he taught. Simply knowing about someone in Church History is already a positive thing because then you are able to compare and see what others are teaching in the history of the Church and see if it is matching up to the Bible and to what the Lord is teaching you and others through His Spirit as we read and study the Scriptures. 

Now let me give you an example. Imagine you are in school and the teacher or professor is teaching a lesson on any subject, lets say it's General Physics and in the span of the entire course, he never once mentions or references the names of Einstein, Galileo, Newton, the very people who founded and shaped the discipline. 

Would it not immediately put into question the validity of the teacher and the teaching when there is no mention of the people who have shaped and founded the understanding of Physics? 

In the same way, when it comes to the teaching and preaching of the Church, whoever is speaking on behalf of the Church, whether that's pastors or preachers, or whether that's you or me, that is exactly what happens and no one questions it. 

For example, you mention Calvin who is from the 16th century. The Church existed for 15 centuries before that. Can you tell me of someone from the first 15 centuries of the History of the Church that you are aware of? Do you think that it is important to know something about the first 15 centuries of the Church? 

I believe in the private study of the Bible and for 18 years have been following the Bible-in-a-year reading plan including daily reading of 2 chapters from the OT and 2 from NT. I have taken the study of the Bible seriously my entire Christian life because I was discipled that way. At one point I was reading 10 chapters a day and memorizing large portions. 

The deeper we study the Bible the more we will ask questions and seek to understand it and naturally that will lead us to consult various people in the Church and Church History. 

I am sure you would agree that studying a book like Revelation would be impossible without consulting some kind of commentaries and I am sure that you have not come up with your understanding of different doctrines solely through your own private interpretation. 

If we seek to understand Scripture with the help of the Holy Spirit and with the help of the Church throughout History whom the Holy Spirit has been guiding, and none of us believes that somehow you or I alone have been uniquely chosen by the Holy Spirit to rightly interpret the Scriptures and that all we need is solely the Bible and our own private interpretation, none of us thinks this way, then my question is, who are we consulting when we seek to interpret the Scriptures in the 2000 years of interpretation and the consensus of the Church throughout the ages? Are we using what the Holy Spirit has provided for us throughout the history of the Church? Would not that be the responsible way to study the Bible? 

When I asked this question to someone, they responded by saying that they study and listen to men much beyond them in biblical maturity. 

I asked them if there were any names that they could mention in particular. My point was that for some strange reason we have a habit to limit "the men we listen to" to a narrow point in history, and yet the Church has existed for a long time. 

Like in the Physics example, we neglect all those men who have contributed throughout Church History. I asked them, are there any people you study and listen to from the first 1000 years of Church History for example? If not, then why not? It is an honest question.
